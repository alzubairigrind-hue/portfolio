# Sadu (السدو) research: honest sourcing for a brass band motif

## 1. Summary

- Al Sadu is a Bedouin women's warp-faced weave. UNESCO inscribed it on the Representative List in 2020 for Saudi Arabia and Kuwait (file 01586). In December 2025 that entry was replaced by file 02158, which adds Qatar.
- The named border motifs are documented in academic sources with Arabic names, construction and placement: العين، الحبوب، الضلعة، ضروس الخيل، العويرجان، المذخر. Documented *meanings* are thin. Most names only describe what the pattern looks like (eye, ribs, horse teeth), and the one symbolic meaning I found (Uwairjan) comes from a single field study.
- Honest sourcing is possible if the page uses a **named border motif** and claims no more meaning than its name. Leave out the shajara field, tribal brands (wasm) and figurative symbols.
- Saudi and Kuwaiti bodies actively promote contemporary and design use. I found no rule or guideline against decorative use.
- Recommended: **Uwairjan (العويرجان)**. Alternatives: **Dhal'a (الضلعة)** and **Dhurus al-khayl (ضروس الخيل)**.

**Definition and technique.** The 2020 nomination form defines the word and the weave:

> "The Word 'Al Sadu' in Arabic means the weaving that is done in a horizontal style … Weaving in principle is a form of warp faced plain weave done by women on a ground loom."

It describes the patterns as "geometric designs based on strips, dots, squares, and triangles, combined to flow on in rhythmic repetition and symmetry", with "bright colors such as reds and oranges".

Source: 2020 nomination form §1(i), https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/47967-EN.doc

**Who practises it**

- According to the nomination form, the primary bearers are "older Bedouin women who are master weavers". Men traditionally shear and clean the wool (§1(ii)).
- Saudi tribes named in the form: Al Beli, Al Juhani, Al Shammar, Al Sharari, Al Anazi, Al Huwaiti, Al Atawi, Al Qahtani, Al Khaldi.
- Kuwaiti tribes named in the form: Ajman, Mutair, Awazim, Reshaida, Shammar (§C).
- Qatar joined in 2025. The families named are Al Marri, Al Naimi and Al Hawajer (2025 form §C).

**Kuwaiti institutions**

- The nomination form says Sadu House (بيت السدو) was created in 1979. The Al Sadu Weaving Cooperative Society, whose shares the weavers own, was founded in 1991 and works from Sadu House (2020 form §C).
- AramcoWorld also says the AlSadu Society was founded in 1979 (https://www.aramcoworld.com/articles/2025/ja25/al-sadu-weaves-stories-of-culture-and-identity).
- Other secondary sources give 1980. Treat the founding year as **UNVERIFIED beyond the nomination form**.

**Saudi institutions**

- The Saudi Heritage Preservation Society gathered the Saudi field data for the 2020 nomination.
- Named Saudi community bodies: Women Cooperative Multipurpose Association (Al Qassim), the Al Kharar development committee (Tabuk), King Abdulaziz Society (Al Jouf) and Desert Designs (Al Khobar) (2020 form §4).

## 3. Motif catalogue

**How the weave is laid out.**

- Weavers make narrow strips and sew them together into larger textiles (Canavan & Alnajadah, http://www.geocities.ws/soascamelconference2011/canavan.pdf, p.83).
- The long central band is the **shajara** (tree). It is done in a complementary-warp, "finger-picked" technique (same source, p.82).
- Narrower warp-patterned bands run along its sides and edges. Plain weave (النسيج المسطح) is the ground between them.

**Caveats from the researchers themselves**

- CAN says motif names were passed down orally, and that "there is great regional variation and multiple terms are frequently used for the same object".
- CAN also warns that interpretations "can only be known and attributed to the actual weaver herself at the time of weaving construction" (pp.84–85).
- All three academic sources are **Kuwaiti**. I found no Saudi-specific motif vocabulary from a primary source.

## 4. Use outside weaving: norms and sensitivity

**No prohibition found**

- I found no UNESCO, Saudi or Kuwaiti rule or guideline restricting decorative or commercial use of Sadu geometry.
- The 2020 nomination form lists contemporary reinterpretation as an intended outcome of inscription:
  - "In Saudi Arabia, fashion designers and artists will be encouraged to incorporate Al Sadu artistically in their designs."
  - Kuwait's annual **SADI (Sadu Art and Design Initiative)** "focuses on the digital interpretation of Al Sadu" (§2).
  - Designers such as Naeema Al Shuhail and Desert Designs are named as safeguarding actors (§3.a).
- Source: https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/47967-EN.doc

**Saudi Ministry of Culture**

- 2025 was the Ministry's **Year of Handicrafts (عام الحرف اليدوية 2025)**. Its mission is «مزاولة الحرف اليدوية، وصونها، واقتنائها، وتوثيق قصصها، وتعزيز حضورها في حياتنا المعاصرة», which includes giving crafts a presence in contemporary life. Sources: https://www.moc.gov.sa/Modules/Pages/Cultural-Years/Year-of-Handicraft and SPA, 1 Jan 2025, https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2236144
- The Ministry's research guide for that year calls Sadu the best-known Saudi textile craft. It says Sadu is women's work through every stage, and that it carries «رموز بصرية من ثقافة الصحراء من حيوانات أو نباتات أو رموز هندسية مجردة». Source: *الدليل المعرفي لعام الحرف اليدوية*, p.36, https://www.moc.gov.sa/-/media/Project/Ministries/Moc/CulturalYears/yoh/MOC_YEAR_OF_HANDCRAFTS_Research_Guide2025_V2.pdf
- SPA (2 Jan 2025) says Sadu "continues to receive significant attention from the Ministry of Culture, the Heritage Commission" and other bodies. Source: https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2236709
- The 2025 extension form reports the following (§3.2): the Royal Institute of Traditional Arts has run a Sadu apprenticeship since 2021 that "merges traditional weave with contemporary creative designs"; Saudi Arabia hosted the handicraft week "Banan" in June 2023; and a Sadu training course in Al-Madinah run with the Ministry of Culture. Source: https://ich.unesco.org/doc/download.php?versionID=81632

**Sensitivity points documented in primary sources**

1. **Motifs can carry tribal identity.**
   - The wasm is a tribal mark (CAN p.83).
   - Kuwaiti students "have more pride in their tribes (after learning about the symbols)" (2020 form §1(iii)).
   - In AramcoWorld, a Sadu Society source says each design "holds meaning, tribal affiliations, marital status, even invocations of protection". That is secondary and not attributed to any specific motif.
   - Simple geometric border bands are the lowest-risk choice. Wasm and shajara content carry the most risk.
2. **Figurative motifs.** CAN notes that the camel symbol "represents a living animal form, which in strict Islamic terms should not be used" (p.84). This is another reason to stay geometric.
3. **Credit to the makers.**
   - The Robertson (2016) paper examines "how sadu symbols are appropriated across various media". Its exhibition was designed to move attention "from symbolic applications to the artisans and heritage of the craft itself" (abstract, https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/1005).
   - **The full text could not be retrieved (403), so its specific recommendations are UNVERIFIED.**
   - The practical takeaway is to name the motif and credit the craft and its weavers, for example in a short caption or footnote, rather than presenting the band as unattributed ornament.
4. **Threat model.** The nomination worries that cheap substitution (cotton for hand-spun wool) will erode knowledge (§3.a). That concern is about the craft, not graphic use. It is not a prohibition.

## 5. Candidate motifs for a thin horizontal brass band

A note on method: the geometry below is my reduction from the *text* descriptions above. I have not checked it against a photographed museum or Sadu House piece. That check is the main open item (§6).

### A. العويرجان Uwairjan (recommended)

- **Meaning.** CAN reports "loftiness or sublimity", with alternative readings of dunes, piles of dates or birds' wings. AJSP offers a tentative etymology from عرجون (date-cluster stalk). Present it as *"reported by Kuwaiti master weavers to signify loftiness"*, not as settled fact.
- **Why it fits.**
  - It is literally *built*: a chain of pyramids assembled dot by dot, and the weaver picks each warp from memory against a ground that always holds. That maps directly to careful construction.
  - AJSP notes the Uwairjan band is woven more densely than plain weave.
  - It has the richest documentation of any strip motif: construction, colours, size range and a reported meaning.
- **Reduction.**
  - Brass square "dots" on walnut, which matches its native scheme of light dots on a black ground.
  - On a 2 px grid, use stepped triangles 3–4 dots tall with bases touching, in one continuous horizontal chain: roughly 6–8 px of motif plus optional 1 px hairlines above and below, 8–12 px in total.
  - At 16 px, add the mirrored row of triangles "facing each other" (ALN).
  - Keep the **stepped dot structure**. A smooth-edged triangle strip loses the Sadu identity.
- **Risk.**
  - The meaning is single-source and plural.
  - At small sizes a stepped triangle row can pass for a generic zigzag. The dot grid is what makes it recognisable.
  - The native version is two-colour (red and white). A single brass colour drops that distinction.

### B. الضلعة Dhal'a (ribs)

- **Meaning.** "Ribs" (AJSP, ALN). CAN adds sand undulations or body parts such as ribs. No deeper symbolism is documented.
- **Why it fits.**
  - Ribs are a frame, the structure under the surface. That fits "careful construction" and plain explanation.
  - Its documented job is exactly this one: **a narrow 1–3 cm band that edges other patterns**, bounded by continuous dark warp lines.
- **Reduction.** Two 1 px brass rules with a row of short, evenly spaced brass bars between them. The bars are the "horizontal ribs" from two-colour warp alternation. It works at 8 px and is the calmest option.
- **Risk.** Of the three, it is the easiest to mistake for generic hatching or a ladder rule. It needs its name in a caption to read as Sadu. The exact rib geometry is **UNVERIFIED** against a physical piece.

### C. ضروس الخيل Dhurus al-khayl (horse teeth)

- **Meaning.** "Horse teeth", resembling large protruding teeth (AJSP). The name is descriptive and there is no symbolism to overclaim, which makes it an honest choice.
- **Why it fits.**
  - It is by function **the border that frames the central pattern** (AJSP), which is what a section divider does.
  - It is built from two interlocked colours with one chosen for the face, which fits the idea of construction you can see.
- **Reduction.** A row of blocky square teeth in brass between hairlines, about 8–12 px.
- **Risk.**
  - The precise tooth geometry is **UNVERIFIED**. The text gives the unit (26 warps × 4 picks) but not the silhouette.
  - Its distinguishing feature, relief on one face, cannot be shown in flat brass.
  - Without a reference photo this is the most likely of the three to be drawn wrong.

**Not recommended for the band.** Shajara is a complex field and not a strip. Wasm is tribal. Mithkhar has no documented meaning. Ragm is a technique rather than a motif. Hubub's meaning is contested, though it is a fine secondary edge line if needed.

## 6. Open questions / unverified items

1. **No visual verification.** I found no museum collection page (for example Sadu House, Kuwait National Museum, the British Museum or the V&A) with an image labelled with one of these motif names. Before drawing, get a labelled photo of Uwairjan, Dhal'a or Dhurus al-khayl from a primary collection, or from Crichton (1989) or Al-Sabah (2006) *Ibjad*.
2. **Saudi-specific names are UNVERIFIED.** All three motif sources are Kuwaiti. CAN notes strong regional variation, so Saudi weavers in Al Jouf, Tabuk or Qassim may use other names.
3. **Contested meanings.**
   - Hubub: seeds (ALN) vs impression of teeth (AJSP).
   - Mithkhar: no meaning found, and the spelling differs (المذخر vs المدخر).
   - Uwairjan's meaning rests on one field study.
   - I found no source giving a meaning for 'Ayn beyond its appearance. Popular claims of an "evil-eye" or protective meaning are **UNVERIFIED** and should not be repeated.
4. **The Robertson (2016) full text was not retrieved.** Its guidance on appropriation is known only from the abstract.
5. **Canavan & Alnajadah (2013)**, "Material Symbols of Traditional Bedouin Al-Sadu Weavings of Kuwait", *TEXTILE* 11(2):152–165 (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2752/175183513X13710294193961), is paywalled and not read. It may hold fuller motif meanings.
6. **"The Pictographic Codes in Al-Sadu Weavings of Kuwait"** on ResearchGate (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369985636) returned 403. Its authors and content are unread.
7. **Heritage Commission.** I found no explicit usage guidance for Sadu from the Heritage Commission (هيئة التراث), only statements of general support via SPA.
8. **Sadu House founding year.** 1979 (nomination form, AramcoWorld) vs 1980 (other secondary sources).
